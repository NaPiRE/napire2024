# List of Hypotheses

This document contains a list of all hypotheses investigated in the scope of the causal analysis of the NaPiRE data.
Hypotheses are based on a pair of variables, the exposure (i.e., the independent variable) and outcome (i.e., the dependent variable).
Where applicable, additional variables are involved in a hypotheses (e.g., mediators and confounders).

## Communication-related Hypotheses

> What factors influence communication in requirements engineering, and how does it shape the downstream process?

The following hypotheses stem from the propositions elicited during the NaPiRE workshop hosted at ISERN'25 (hypotheses in quotation marks were proposed by workshop participants verbatim).
These hypotheses involve at least one variable of type "Communication".

| ID | Source | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| $H_1^{P1}$ | P1 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer impacts whether the project leader decides that the team has spent enough time on requirements analysis. |
| $H_2^{P1}$ | P1 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer impacts whether the project leader decides that the team has wasted enough time on requirements analysis. |
| $H_1^{P2}$ | P2 | Teams of different size have different communication frequency. |
| $H_2^{P2}$ | P2 | Communication frequency impacts that changes in requirements are not communicated. | 
| $H_3^{P2}$ | P2 | Higher communication frequency reduces the number of communication flaws within the team. |
| $H_4^{P2}$ | P2 | "The more you communicate the longer time you spend". |
| $H_5^{P2}$ | P2 | "The more you communicate the higher the chance to waste time". |
| $H_6^{P2}$ | P2 | Communication frequency impacts the likelihood of teams agreeing that they need to start now otherwise they would not finish in time. |
| $H_1^{P4}$ | P4 | Incomplete or hidden requirements impact the quality of communication inside a team. |
| $H_2^{P4}$ | P4 | The quality of communication between the team and other teams on the same projects causes requirements to be incomplete or hidden. |
| $H_3^{P4}$ | P4 | The relationship between the project team and its customer causes requirements to be incomplete or hidden. |
| $H_4^{P4}$ | P4 | Langauge barriers affect the quality of communication within a team and between a team and other teams on the project. |
| $H_1^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause communication flaws. |
| $H_2^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause that changes in requirements are not communicated |
| $H_3^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause weak access to customer needs and business information. |

## Problem-causing Hypotheses

> What factors of requirements engineering cause or mitigate downstream problems?

The following hypotheses stem from the propositions elicited during the NaPiRE workshop hosted at ISERN'25.
In all of these hypotheses, the outcome variable is of type "Problem".

| ID | Source | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| $H_1^{P2}$ | P2 | Communication frequency impacts that changes in requirements are not communicated. |
| $H_{1b}^{P2}$ | self | The way-of-working moderates how communication frequency impacts that changes in requirements are not communicated . |
| $H_2^{P2}$ | P2 | Communication frequency cause communication flaws within our project team. |
| $H_3^{P2}$ | P2 | If the project members collectively agreed that "if we don’t start implementation now, we would not finish implementation in time", this causes a lack of trust between our project team members and stakeholder or customer. |
| $H_1^{P3}$ | P3 | The team or project leader deciding that the team has wasted enough time on requirements analysis causes underspecified requirements. |
| $H_2^{P3}$ | P3 | The team or project leader deciding that the team has wasted enough time on requirements analysis causes perceiving insufficient support by the project lead. |
| $H_1^{P4}$ | P4 | Not having enough time to carry out tasks causes requirements to be unclear or unmeasurable. |
| $H_2^{P4}$ | P4 | Not having enough time to carry out tasks causes important stakeholders not to be represented in the project. |
| $H_3^{P4}$ | P4 | Important stakeholders not being represented in the project produces unclear or unmeasurable requirements. |
| $H_4^{P4}$ | P4 | Important stakeholders not being represented in the project produces hidden or incomplete requirements. |
| $H_5^{P4}$ | P4 | Not having enough time to carry out tasks causes requirements to be hidden or incomplete. |
| $H_6^{P4}$ | P4 | Language barriers cause unclear or unmeasurable requirements. |
| $H_7^{P4}$ | P4 | The quality of communication between the team and other teams on the same projects causes requirements to be incomplete or hidden. |
| $H_1^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause communication flaws. |
| $H_2^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause that changes in requirements are not communicated. |
| $H_3^{P6}$ | P6 | The relationship between the project's team and its customer can cause weak access to customer needs and business information. |
| $H_4^{P6}$ | P6 | When the project members collectively agree that if they do not start implementation now, they would not finish implementation in time, it can cause gold plating. |
| $H_5^{P6}$ | P6 | When the project members collectively agree that if they do not start implementation now, they would not finish implementation in time, it can cause weak access to customer needs and business information. |
| $H_6^{P6}$ | P6 | When the project members collectively agree that if they do not start implementation now, they would not finish implementation in time, it may leaf insufficient time to carry out tasks. |
